    3.1 Build Mission Control: an app for the morning after (3:35, 15 min)¶

    Goal

    Build a Studio app from a design file, open it, and replay the night: restart your attack and watch your agent handle it again, live, with a stopwatch.

    Your agent works at night. In the morning, the day shift wants to know three things: what happened, can I trust what the agent did, and what does it mean for the business? An agent answers in a report. An app answers on one screen, for anyone, every morning. Studio builds both: an agent decides and acts, an app shows and lets people act.

    Agent (Part 2) App (Part 3)
    Runs On a schedule, by itself When someone opens it
    Decides Yes: what is wrong, what to do No: it shows data and offers buttons
    Built from A description in plain words A design file (Markdown), so anyone can rebuild the same app
    Here Finds, stops and clears your attack Shows the timeline, the evidence, the business impact and your group

    1. Add the Night Watch Console integration (3 min)¶

    The app reads your seat's timeline from a small API made for it. Add it the same way as Path A in 2.3:

    1. Studio → Build → Integrations → Add → REST API → Use URL and paste:

      Value
      Night Watch Console spec URL <your Night Watch Console spec URL>

    2. Review API: 7 operations. Three are for the core app: get_my_timeline, get_my_group_board, replay_my_attack. Four are read-only, for the add-ons: get_my_security_events, get_business_services, get_oncall, get_recent_changes.

    3. Configure: Display name Night Watch Console, Bearer token .
    4. Review and save → Save draft, then click Manage in the pop-up.

    Why a separate integration

    Your agent must never restart its own attack, so replay_my_attack lives only here, where only the app can use it. The four read-only operations are the same data your agent reads through Lab Tools, served on the standard HTTPS port: Studio apps reach APIs on port 443 only. The app never gets stop_my_lane; containment stays the agent's job.

    2. Download the design file (1 min)¶

    Download NightWatch_Mission_Control.md

    It is plain Markdown: what the app is for, the rules it must follow, the data contract (the exact fields each operation returns, so Studio does not have to guess), and four sections. Studio builds A (your night: status, response times, timeline, your group, Replay the night), B (the evidence your agent saw) and C (business impact) in one go. D is an add-on you can ask for afterwards. A design file is how you make an app repeatable: the next person gets the same app from the same file.

    3. Build the app from the file (6 min)¶

    1. Studio → Build → App → Build.

    2. Click + in the box, attach NightWatch_Mission_Control.md, and type:

      Build the attached design now, without asking questions. Use my integration Night Watch Console.

    3. Send it and wait. Studio checks the integration and all seven operations the design names, builds the app, then runs its own checks (styling, types, tests) and fixes what fails. This takes about 5 minutes; do not send another message while it works.

    4. Name it yours. Studio names the app after your prompt, so every app in the room starts with the same name. Select the pencil next to the title at the top. In Edit App details:

      Field Value
      (1) App name <your seat> Mission Control
      (2) Description Night Watch Mission Control for seat <your seat>

      (3) Select Save changes. The title at the top now shows your seat.

    Studio asks one question first

    Before it builds, Studio's planner usually stops to confirm one detail (replay timing, the theme, a refresh rate). That is normal. Reply Use your recommendation and build it. and wait again. The one exception: if it offers to hide your seat or watch source, answer Show them exactly as returned.

    If you see This change didn't complete

    Studio restores the last good version. Send the same prompt again; if it fails twice, ask for Build section A of the attached design. first, then Add sections B and C of the design.

    4. Open it and read your night (3 min)¶

    When the build finishes, the app opens in Preview on the right.

    1. Your app's name, with your seat.
    2. Header: your seat, the service you protect, your watch source, your card state and when it last refreshed.
    3. Replay the night (step 5).
    4. Response times: Time to detect, Time to contain and Time to resolve, or not yet.
    5. Install would add the app to your Apps menu. Skip it for this lab: use the app here in Preview.

    Use the app in Preview

    At the moment an installed Studio app cannot reach REST integrations in this tenant: its panels say unavailable while the same app works in the builder Preview. Everything in this lab works in Preview.

    Scroll down:

    • Timeline: when your attack started, your agent's alert in its own words, the stop and the clear. Steps that have not happened yet are greyed.
    • My group: the five seats in your group, yours marked You.

    • Evidence my agent saw: (1) the top destinations your source talked to in the last hour, (2) connections, IPS and file events in the last 5 minutes. After your agent stops your attack, a green Quiet since badge appears.

    • Business impact: (1) your service: criticality, segment, apps and the impact if it goes down; (2) who is on call and the wake policy.

    • (1) Recent changes in the last 24 hours. (2) same segment marks a change on your service's segment: the first place to look when something breaks.

    No business context?

    Only some lab services have a business-context record. If My service says No business context for this service, the app is working; on call and recent changes still show.

    5. Replay the night (3 min)¶

    1. In the app, select Replay the night. (1) Read what it does, then (2) select Replay the night.

    2. The app goes (1) amber, Attack running, not reported yet, and (2) Live, with a stopwatch.

    3. In Studio, schedule your agent for the next 5-minute slot (Manage → your agent → Edit schedule), as in 2.4. Then hands off: when the run starts, the timeline fills in step by step (alert, contained, resolved) and the card goes red, then Verifying, then green.

    4. (1) Response times fill in. In this run: 8:15 to detect (mostly waiting for the scheduled slot), 0:14 to contain, 8:44 to resolve.

    5. (2) The Timeline shows each step, with your agent's alert in its own words.
    6. (3) Your card in My group turns Resolved, with your agent's summary. In live mode the app also shows "Your agent handled it in m:ss".

    6. Add-on: shift handover notes (if you have time)¶

    Add section D of the design.

    A note box your whole group shares: what your agent found, for the day shift. Notes are stored in the app, so everyone in your group sees them.

    What you just did

    You built a second kind of Studio application from a file, on top of the same integrations your agent uses. The agent does the night shift; the app is how people see and trust it. Change the design file (another panel, another integration) and build again: the file is the source code.
