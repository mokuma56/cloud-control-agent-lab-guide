    Build a Network Agent in Cisco Cloud Control That Troubleshoots Anything While You Sleep

    LTROPS-2623 · Cisco Live EMEA 2027 · 4-hour instructor-led lab · 30 seats

    You are the night-shift engineer at PseudoCo, and you would like to sleep tonight. Your table looks after one PseudoCo service: the Employee Intranet, the directory servers, the partner portal and so on. Its column on the room wall is headed with that service. In the next four hours you teach Cisco Cloud Control to do your night shift. It has to find what is hurting the business across every Cisco domain, add the business context only you know, decide how bad it is, wake you only when it matters, take one safe action you approve, and keep the service desk ticket up to date so the day shift can trust it.

    Tonight: two incidents, one method

    Incident 1: a change breaks the branch Incident 2: something hits your service
    When Part 1, AI Canvas Part 2, Lights Out
    Who The whole room, one shared fault Every seat its own, at the same time
    Who drives You: you troubleshoot in Canvas and refine the prompt Your agent: you are "asleep" while it finds, stops and clears the attack
    What it really is A port change on a branch switch (users get no IP address) Hostile traffic against your table's service
    The ticket A user calls the service desk; you write the root cause into their ticket A user calls, or your agent finds it first; your agent writes the root cause into the ticket and resolves it

    The two incidents have nothing in common, on purpose. In Part 1 you do not write a "Leaf1 prompt". You write a general triage method: look across every Cisco domain, check what changed, add the business context, then report in a fixed format. In 1.4 you save that method, and in Part 2 it becomes your agent's instructions. At Lights Out nobody tells your agent that tonight's problem is an attack. The same method that found a bad switch change has to find it on its own. That is what troubleshoots anything means.

    The lab follows three steps:

    1. Test a multi-domain troubleshooting prompt in AI Canvas on incident 1.
    2. Save it as a general triage method, together with the answer that proves it works.
    3. Build an agent in Cloud Control Studio from that method, with a custom workflow of your own: an integration that brings in non-Cisco information and lights up your seat on the room wall. Then let it handle incident 2 while you sleep.

    What you leave with

    A working Studio agent, a Canvas prompt you trust, an integration pattern (MCP or REST) you can point at any system your business depends on, and the take-home kit to rebuild it.

    • AI Canvas does the Cisco multi-domain work with its built-in integrations (Meraki, Secure Firewall, Secure Access, Catalyst Center, Intersight, Nexus Dashboard).
    • Lab Tools Production is the lab team's custom integration. It brings in what Cisco cannot know: PseudoCo's change calendar, business services, on-call policy and SaaS status. It also gives your agent its watch scope, the firewall events for your seat, and two actions: raise your seat's alert on the room wall, and stop your seat's attack traffic.
    • PseudoCo Service Desk is PseudoCo's ticket system (it works like an ITSM such as ServiceNow). Each seat has its own queue, opened with Log in to Service Desk on your seat card. Users' calls arrive as tickets; Canvas and your agent write the root cause into them, open tickets when they find a problem first, and resolve them. Only you, as the caller, close a ticket.
    • Open-Meteo is a public weather API with no credential. Your agent adds the weather at its site to its evidence: outside context no Cisco product has.
    • My Lab Tools is your integration, built in 2.3 (REST or MCP) with your own rules. Your agent switches to it to raise and clear its alert: your own workflow.
    • Lanes: in Part 2 the traffic generator sends one stream of simulated attack traffic per seat, called a lane. Each lane comes from that seat's own source IP and goes through the firewall to the service its table protects.
    • Your seat token is the key. Everyone uses the same URLs; the token decides which seat, which lane and which light you can see and touch. You can never act on another seat.

    PseudoCo Service Desk: what you see

    PseudoCo's IT service desk is where users report problems. When someone calls, the service desk opens an incident ticket. In this lab every seat has its own ticket queue: your seat token decides which queue you see, and you can never see or change another seat's tickets.

    It works like an ITSM system such as ServiceNow, with the same ideas and field names, so what you practice here carries over to your own company's ticket system.

    Who does what

    Who Does Where
    A caller (the proctor plays them) Reports a problem. The ticket arrives vague, the way users write Your queue fills by itself in Part 1 and at Lights Out
    You, in Canvas (Part 1) Find the root cause and write it into the caller's ticket 1.3 Work the ticket
    Your agent (Part 2) Reads your queue every run. Works a ticket a person opened, or opens its own when it finds a problem first. Writes a note after each action and resolves the ticket once the fix is verified 2.1 rules, 2.2 and 2.4 tests, 2.5 Lights Out
    You, as the caller Read the result and close the ticket, or reopen it if the handover is not good enough My tickets

    Your agent can resolve, but only you can close

    Closing a ticket is the caller confirming the problem is really gone. The Service Desk API that your agent uses has no close operation at all, so the limit is built into the system, not only written in a prompt.

    Open your queue

    Select Log in to Service Desk on your seat card: . Your queue, My tickets, opens in a new tab and refreshes by itself every few seconds, so keep it open next to Canvas or Studio.

    The page

    A ticket after Canvas worked it in 1.3: (1) the ticket in your queue, with priority and state, (2) the priority Canvas set from the business impact, (3) the change that caused it, (4) the comment for the caller, (5) the internal work note with the full STATUS report.

    On the left: your queue, newest first. Each row shows:

    • the ticket number (INC00100xx), its priority (P1 to P4) and state;
    • the short description, in the caller's words;
    • who opened it: a caller's name, or Opened by your agent. Tickets your agent opens during sandbox tests are marked test run and are cleared before Lights Out.

    On the right: the selected ticket.

    Field Means
    State New → In progress → Resolved → Closed (On hold means waiting for information)
    Priority P1 (urgent) to P4, from impact and urgency. Your agent sets it from its SEVERITY: critical = P1, major = P2, minor = P3
    Service The PseudoCo business service affected (cmdb_ci)
    Assignment group The team that owns the fix, for example Network Operations or Security Operations
    Caller Who reported it, or Your agent
    Caused by change The change number from the change calendar, set only when the evidence matches
    Parent incident / Grouped under it One incident with several callers: the extra tickets are grouped under one parent, and resolving the parent resolves them all

    Below the fields: the caller's own description, then the timeline, newest first. It has three kinds of entry:

    Entry Who reads it What belongs in it
    Work note (internal) The IT team The technical report: STATUS, CAUSE, EVIDENCE with sources, NEXT STEP, and one note per action (alert raised, source stopped, verification totals)
    Comment (caller can see) The caller Plain language, no IP addresses or VLANs: what is wrong, what is being done, what to do meanwhile
    Activity Everyone What changed on the ticket, written by the system: priority, group, state, who closed it

    When a ticket is resolved

    (1) State Resolved, (2) the close code and close notes: what fixed it and how it was verified, (3) Confirm and close, (4) Reopen, (5) the activity that resolved it (here your 1.4 re-check, after the back-out of the Part 1 change).

    Read the close notes and the timeline the way the day shift would, then:

    • Confirm and close if you agree it is fixed and the handover is good enough;
    • Reopen and say what is missing if it is not. Your comment goes into the timeline and your agent reads it on its next run.

    While a ticket is open you can also Add comment as the caller, for example to add a detail. Your agent reads comments on its next run.

    The room wall

    There are no lights on the tables. Instead, the room screen shows the Night Watch wall: all 30 seats, one column per table, headed by the service that table protects. Your seat ID is T<table>-<letter>, for example T3-C is table 3, seat C. The small colored dot on your tile matches your seat card.

    (1) Amber: a seat whose traffic is running and that no agent has reported yet, (2) red: an agent raised its alert, with its summary, (3) the ticket the agent is working, (4) tickets open in the PseudoCo Service Desk, (5) known change requests from the change calendar (last 24 hours and scheduled), (6) what the agents said. A tile goes striped while its agent verifies, then green when resolved. There is no leaderboard: agents run at different times.

    Your tile Means Who changes it
    Grey, Quiet Nothing is happening at your seat -
    Amber, Threat active · undetected Your lane is running and no agent has reported it yet the proctor starts traffic after the break; you restart your own
    Ticket number on the tile (for example INC0010042 · resolved) Your seat's current ticket in PseudoCo Service Desk your agent and the service desk
    Red, Alert + your agent's summary Your agent called signal_issue. Minor is steady, major pulses, critical pulses fast your agent
    Red stripes, Stopped · verifying Your agent called stop_my_lane on your own source and has not confirmed it is quiet yet your agent
    Green, Resolved ✓ Your agent verified the source is quiet and called clear_issue your agent

    When every open seat at a table is resolved, ALL CLEAR pops up over that table for a few seconds. It is not a race: agents run at different times, so tiles change at different times.
